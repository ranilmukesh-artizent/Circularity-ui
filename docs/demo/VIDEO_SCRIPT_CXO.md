# Video script: the CXO demo (draft 1, 28 Oct 2026)

For senior executives (CEO, COO, CFO, CIO; 50+, decades in operations). Plan and research: [`../PLAN_CXO_DEMO_VIDEO.md`](../PLAN_CXO_DEMO_VIDEO.md). **Recorded 28 Oct 2026** as `video/circularity-cxo-demo.mp4`. The spoken text of record is [`video/pipeline/narration_cxo.json`](video/pipeline/narration_cxo.json); it differs from draft 1 below in x0 (shorter), x1 (Planner sign-in), x2a (64 for Customer A), x4e (the planner submits), x5 (a second person), x6a (order management, carrier), x7 (the worn shaft sleeve), x8d and x9b. Legal should review the final wording before it is shown outside the team.

## How it should sound

The voice is a senior operator who has run workshops and asset fleets for forty years. He is explaining something useful to peers, not selling to them.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Short, declarative sentences. One idea each | Stacked adjectives, "seamless", "cutting-edge", "revolutionary", "game-changer" |
| Numbers, said plainly, then a pause | "AI-powered", "autonomous", "magic", "next-generation" |
| Say what the agents **did**: "pulled", "priced", "checked", "drafted", "mapped" | Say what agents **are**: "our intelligent agents leverage…" |
| Give the person the decision: "your planner makes the call", "nothing leaves until someone signs" | Imply the system acts alone on money or customers |
| Business nouns: order, workshop, work order, certificate, margin, purchase avoided | Tech nouns: API, schema, pipeline, LLM, canonical (say "one shared model") |
| Name system **types**: ERP, order management, MRO workshop, warehouse | Name vendors or products |
| Understate. "That is the point." "Nothing hidden." | Superlatives, exclamation, rhetorical questions in a row |

Pace: about 140 words a minute, a beat after every figure. Target **≈ 700 words → ≈ 5:00 of speech, ≈ 5:30 with the screens' pauses**.

## Script

`[screen]` is what is on screen while the line plays. Words in *italics* are spelled for the voice in `narration_cxo.json` (E R P, M R O, amounts in words).

### 0 · Opening (DI landing) · 0:00–0:12 · 32 words

`[framework/index.html hero; the cursor rests on Recover, Resolve, Reconcile in turn; click Recover]`

> **x0** One decision platform, three applications. Recover finds the best next life for every returned asset. Resolve coordinates the action across teams. Reconcile checks the evidence before anyone sends an invoice. Let's start with Recover.

### 1 · Sign in · 0:12–0:20 · 17 words

`[sign-in; typing cut; caption ADMIN]`

> **x1** I'm signed in as an administrator, so we can follow one asset from start to finish.

### 2 · Returned assets and the asset · 0:20–0:42 · 56 words

`[Returned assets: the 128 tile, then RT-1048 "Start here"; open the asset; photo lightbox for 3 s; health ring]`

> **x2a** This month a hundred and twenty-eight assets came back. Every one of them is a decision with money attached.
>
> **x2b** Take this one. A fifty-five kilowatt motor, back from a lease. Its owner, its grade, its certificate and its history are already here, drawn from the systems that hold them. Nobody keyed any of it in.

### 3 · Stages 1–5: arrive and evidence · 0:42–1:05 · 62 words

`[Case overview: the ten-stage map with stages 1–6 done → stage 1 page → stage 5 page, Photos tab]`

> **x3a** The journey has ten stages. The first five happened before anyone had to look at it. The lease ended, the return was authorised, the motor was collected and received.
>
> **x3b** Then the M R O workshop inspected it and graded it. At each stage an agent fetched the record from the system that owns it, and checked nothing was missing.

### 4 · Stage 6: the decision, why, evidence, what-if · 1:05–2:05 · 150 words

`[stage 6: recommendation card; then the Explain tab: why on the left, evidence snapshot on the right]`

> **x4a** Stage six is the decision. The agents priced seven possible routes: redeploy, resell, re-lease, harvest parts, recycle. One was ruled out on eligibility before price came into it.
>
> **x4b** The recommendation is to redeploy it against this customer's replacement order. *Ninety-six hundred dollars* net. *Forty-eight hundred* better than selling it.

`[cursor down the drivers; evidence rail items light up as each driver is named]`

> **x4c** And here is why. It saves buying a new motor for that order. The refurbishment is modest. And the certificate covers the full term the customer needs. On the right is the evidence it used: each figure, the system it came from, and when.

`[slider: Preparation, parts and fees → +30 %; result card: still first. Then preset Demand cancelled: ranking flips]`

> **x4d** Now stress it. Put the workshop cost up thirty percent. Redeploy still wins. Cancel the customer's order, and the ranking flips: selling becomes the better route, and it tells you by how much.
>
> **x4e** That is the point. You see the reasoning, not just the answer.

### 5 · Stage 7: a person approves · 2:05–2:25 · 44 words

`[stage 7 → Approve → approval card: drafted rationale, limit, the instruction; tick; Approve and send]`

> **x5** Stage seven is where a person decides. The agent has drafted the rationale. The approver reads it, checks it is within their limit, and signs. Nothing goes to an operating system until that happens.

### 6 · Stages 8–9: the work goes to the systems that do it · 2:25–2:55 · 63 words

`[stage 8: handoff rows acknowledged one by one: E R P order, M R O work order, warehouse transfer; stage 9: outcome]`

> **x6a** Then the work goes to the systems that do the work. The E R P raises the replacement order. The M R O workshop gets a refurbishment work order: repair, bench test, certify. The warehouse moves it to the customer's site.
>
> **x6b** Each system sends its reference back. So every step can be traced, and every cost lands where it belongs.

### 7 · Stage 10: reconcile and learn · 2:55–3:15 · 46 words

`[stage 10: predicted vs actual by cost line; each line names the model that learns]`

> **x7** Stage ten closes the loop. Predicted, *ninety-six hundred*. Actual, *ninety-two thirty*. The difference is explained line by line, and each line goes back to the model that made the estimate. The next decision starts a little better informed.

### 8 · Connection Fabric: plug and play · 3:15–4:20 · 150 words

`[Fabric Overview: 20 connected]`

> **x8a** None of this needed a new E R P or a new workshop system. The Connection Fabric sits on top of what you already run. Twenty systems, connected as they are.

`[Source Map → Source Tables, ✦ Suggest links → Canonical Models]`

> **x8b** Here is how their records come together into one shared picture of an asset, its condition, the demand for it and the decision. The agents read the tables and proposed these links. Your team reviews them, and can change any of them by hand.

`[Workflow Studio → Ask: "Draft a workflow that brings repair and test results in from the MRO workshop every 15 minutes" → draft card → Accept → the canvas builds step by step]`

> **x8c** New connections work the same way. I ask for one in plain words. The agent drafts the steps and checks them. I accept, and there it is.

`[Ask on the canvas: "Run it every five minutes, and flag any unit that fails its bench test" → ghost step and changed trigger → Apply → v0.2]`

> **x8d** Change it the same way. Every five minutes, and flag any unit that fails its bench test. The change is shown before it is made. A person still publishes it.

### 9 · Analytics: does it work? · 4:20–4:50 · 66 words

`[Predicted vs actual scatter and Model versions side by side; ✦ → agent panel beside the chart]`

> **x9a** And how do you know it works? Every decision is held to account. Each dot is one decision: what we predicted, against what actually happened.
>
> **x9b** With each version of the model the error comes down, from about eighteen percent to twelve and a half. Ask the agent beside the chart what changed, and it shows you the cases behind it.

### 10 · Close · 4:50–5:20 · 72 words

`[close card; then 2 s on the DI landing page]`

> **x10a** So, to sum up. It works wherever assets come back, in any industry. It doesn't replace your E R P, your order management or your M R O workshop. It sits on top of them and uses what they already know.
>
> **x10b** The agents do the groundwork: the evidence, the options, the paperwork. Your people make the decisions. And every decision gets a little better than the last.
